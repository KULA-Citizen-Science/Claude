# An ADHD-Grounded Task Taxonomy for Obsidian: Categorizing, Rating, and Initiating Tasks Across Work and Daily Life

## TL;DR
- The strongest evidence-based scaffolding for an ADHD task system comes from three peer-reviewed pillars — Barkley's self-regulation/time-blindness model, the executive-function targets in Solanto's and Safren's manualized CBT (both RCT-supported), and the documented time-estimation/planning-fallacy deficit — while the popular "task-type" language (Dodson's INCUP, "Wall of Awful," dopamine menus) is useful clinical heuristic, not validated science.
- The most defensible design categorizes each task along a handful of dimensions that map to *known deficits* — activation energy/initiation difficulty, executive-function demand, time estimate + a buffer multiplier, energy/cognitive load, structure (defined vs. open-ended), and interest/urgency — and pairs each with an externalized time cue and a "next physical action."
- Operationalized in Obsidian TaskNotes, this becomes a controlled vocabulary of tags plus user-defined frontmatter fields (`timeEstimate`, `effort`, `activation`, `energy`, EF-demand) and a capture→triage→initiate workflow that forces task breakdown, an achievability rating, and a time estimate at the moment of capture.

## Key Findings

**1. Executive dysfunction is the organizing construct, and it implies task dimensions.** Barkley's theory (Barkley, 1997, "ADHD, self-regulation, and time: toward a more comprehensive theory," and expanded in *Executive Functions: What They Are, How They Work, and Why They Evolved*, Guilford, 2012) frames ADHD as a disorder of self-regulation in which behavior falls under the control of "the temporal now" rather than internally represented information about past and future — the root of "temporal myopia"/time blindness. Brown's six-cluster model (Activation, Focus, Effort, Emotion, Memory, Action) explicitly names task-relevant sub-skills: Activation = "organizing tasks and materials, estimating time, prioritizing tasks, and getting started on work tasks." These clusters are directly usable as tag dimensions.

**2. Manualized CBT provides the evidence-based task operations.** Solanto's Meta-Cognitive Therapy — tested in an RCT of 88 clinically referred adults (Solanto et al., 2010, *American Journal of Psychiatry* 167(8):958–968), where the MCT group improved 5.0 points on the AISRS inattentive subscale versus 2.3 in controls (a 2.7-point between-group advantage) — and Safren's *Mastering Your Adult ADHD* (2nd ed., 2017), whose RCT of 86 medication-treated adults (Safren et al., 2010, *JAMA* 304(8):875–880) achieved a ≥30% symptom reduction in "more than two thirds of the CBT group but in only one third of the relaxation group," both target time management, organization, and planning. Safren's core modules include "Organization of Multiple Tasks," "Problem-Solving and Managing Overwhelming Tasks," and A/B/C priority ratings.

**3. Time estimation is measurably impaired — justifying a buffer multiplier.** Marx et al. (2022, *Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry*, "Meta-analysis: Altered Perceptual Timing Abilities in ADHD"), screening 2,266 records and meta-analyzing 55 studies, found "meta-analytic evidence of a broad range of timing deficits" across time discrimination, estimation, production, and reproduction, "indicative of an accelerated internal clock." Zheng, Wang, Chiu & Shum (2022, *Journal of Attention Disorders* 26(2)), reviewing 27 studies (1,620 ADHD vs. 1,249 controls), found people with ADHD "perceived time less accurately (Hedges' g > 0.40), less precisely (Hedges' g = 0.66) and had higher tendency to overestimate time." The planning fallacy (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) is amplified in ADHD; the practitioner rule-of-thumb is a 1.5× multiplier, using percentage buffers rather than fixed additions.

**4. The popular "task-type" frameworks are heuristics, not validated science.** Dodson's interest-based nervous system and ICNU motivators come from a 2006 *Psychiatric Times* trade article ("Real-World Office Management of ADHD in Adults," based on a 2005 conference talk) and a 2018 ADDitude piece, not peer-reviewed research; Dodson disclosed pharmaceutical consulting relationships. Mahan's "Wall of Awful" is a coaching metaphor (©2016, ADHD Essentials; book forthcoming 2026). These are still practically useful for tagging interest/novelty/urgency and emotional-barrier dimensions.

**5. Externalizing time and next-actions is the highest-leverage intervention.** Barkley's "point of performance" and "make time external/physical" principles, implementation intentions (Gollwitzer's if-then plans), and radical task chunking ("next smallest step") all converge on the same design: put the cue, the timer, and the tiny next action where the work happens.

## Details

### 1. Academic/clinical models implying task dimensions

**Russell Barkley — self-regulation and time blindness (peer-reviewed, foundational).** In Barkley (1997) and the hybrid model in *ADHD and the Nature of Self-Control*, behavioral inhibition enables four executive functions (nonverbal working memory, internalized speech, self-regulation of affect/motivation/arousal, and reconstitution) that bring behavior "under the control of time." ADHD returns control "to the temporal now." [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00122/pdf) He coined "temporal myopia," later "time blindness," describing nearsightedness to future events. [ReachLink](https://reachlink.com/advice/adhd/adhd-time-blindness/) His practical corollaries — externalize information, make time physical, arrange external motivation at the "point of performance," and reduce the time gaps between task, deadline, and consequence — are the design principles for any ADHD task system. Evidence strength: strong theoretical model with extensive empirical support; the specific interventions are evidence-informed clinical recommendations.

**Thomas Brown — six-cluster executive function model (clinical framework).** Brown's model (activation, focus, effort, emotion, memory, action) is operationalized in the Brown Executive Function/Attention Scales. [CBDV](https://neurolaunch.com/brown-executive-function-attention-scales/) The Activation cluster maps directly onto task initiation, time estimation, prioritization, and organizing materials. [Brown ADHD Clinic](https://www.brownadhdclinic.com/brown-ef-model-adhd) Brown stresses the clusters "operate in an integrated way." Evidence strength: clinically validated assessment instrument; the model is descriptive/clinical rather than an experimentally derived causal theory.

**Steven Safren et al. — *Mastering Your Adult ADHD* CBT (peer-reviewed, RCT-supported).** The program (developed at MGH/Harvard) has core modules: Module 1 Organizing and Planning (foundation skills; organization of multiple tasks; problem-solving and managing overwhelming tasks; organizational systems), Module 2 Reducing Distractibility (gauging attention span, breaking tasks to fit attention span, modifying the environment), Module 3 Adaptive Thinking (cognitive restructuring), and an optional procrastination module. It uses a task list + calendar system with priority ratings and breaking down large tasks.

**Mary Solanto — Meta-Cognitive Therapy (peer-reviewed, RCT-supported).** MCT is a manualized group CBT extending CBT to executive self-management, targeting time management, organization, and planning. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054707305100) Solanto's 2025 *World Psychiatry* review lists task-related targets: "failure to complete tasks; difficulty with organization; avoidance of tasks requiring sustained mental effort; losing things; and forgetting things." Evidence: among the strongest available for adult ADHD psychosocial treatment.

**Overall CBT evidence.** A review (PMC12434339) notes Safren's two RCTs yielded "moderate effect sizes of 0.52 and 0.58… favoring CBT-ADHD," and a 2025 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (*Journal of Affective Disorders*) found SMD −0.45 for core symptoms and −0.43 for executive function (pure CBT: −0.51 and −0.56, respectively). Earlier meta-analyses (e.g., PubMed 27554190) found CBT superior to waitlist (SMD = 0.76) and to active controls (SMD = 0.43).

**Task initiation, procrastination, delay aversion (peer-reviewed).** Sonuga-Barke's dual-pathway model (2002, *Behavioural Brain Research*; 2003) posits two dissociable deficits — executive dysfunction and delay aversion (a motivational style to escape/avoid delay) — later extended to a triple-pathway including temporal processing (Sonuga-Barke, Bitsakou & Thompson, 2010, *JAACAP*). [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43247877_Beyond_the_Dual_Pathway_Model_Evidence_for_the_Dissociation_of_Timing_Inhibitory_and_Delay-Related_Impairments_in_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder) Procrastination is disproportionately common in adult ADHD and mediates reduced quality of life (adult-sample research in *PMC12423737*). Temporal discounting (steeper in ADHD) explains why immediate rewards dominate over delayed ones.

**Time estimation / prospective timing deficits (peer-reviewed).** Beyond Marx et al. (2022) and Zheng et al. (2022) above, this is the empirical basis for not trusting internal time estimates and for externalizing and buffering time.

### 2. Explicit task taxonomies / dimensional schemes

**Dodson's interest-based nervous system / ICNU / INCUP (practitioner heuristic).** Coined by William Dodson in *Psychiatric Times* (2006) and popularized in ADDitude (2018). Dodson's original acronym was **ICNU — Interest, Competition (Challenge), Novelty, Urgency**. **INCUP** adds Passion (Interest, Novelty, Challenge, Urgency, Passion), and **PINCH** = Passion/Play, Interest, Novelty, Competition/Cooperation, Hurry — later restatements of the same construct. Dodson also recommends rotating "60–100 different techniques" so that old ones feel novel again. Evidence: explicitly a clinical/practitioner heuristic — a "working heuristic, not a published neuroscientific dichotomy," loosely consistent with (but not validated by) dopamine/reward research (Volkow et al., 2009/2011). Still useful as tag dimensions (interest, novelty, urgency) because they capture real motivational levers.

**Wall of Awful (Brendan Mahan — coaching metaphor).** The emotional barrier from repeated failure that blocks initiation, with five response styles (staring at the wall and going around it don't work; "Hulk Smash" works but is unhealthy; climbing the wall and putting a door in it work). Useful for an "emotional-barrier" flag. No peer review.

**Effort / cognitive load / structure / activation energy / duration (mixed).** These dimensions recur in ADHD coaching, occupational therapy, and CBT literature. "Activation energy" (the surge needed to start) and "next smallest step" chunking are widely used. [Positive Reset Eatontown](https://positivereseteatontown.com/task-initiation-adhd-understanding-the-science-behind-why-starting-feels-impossible/) [Busy Brains Coaching](https://www.busybrainscoaching.com/blog/why-task-activation-is-so-hard-7-science-backed-strategies-for-adhd) Occupational therapy for adult ADHD (UK Adult ADHD Network consensus statement, *BMC Psychiatry* 2021) analyzes the mismatch between executive/attention/sensory capacity and task demands, and uses graded skill-building and habituation. [Kantoko](https://www.kantoko.com.au/articles/occupational-therapy-adhd) [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-021-03070-z) No single validated dimensional grid exists; the evidence-based literature (Solanto; Ramsay & Rostain) sorts tasks by executive-function demand and priority/avoidance rather than by a novelty/interest grid.

### 3. Task breakdown, achievability, and time-estimation interventions

- **Chunking / next-action granularity:** Break tasks "to an almost ridiculous degree"; the "next smallest step" removes the activation barrier. [Positive Reset Eatontown](https://positivereseteatontown.com/task-initiation-adhd-understanding-the-science-behind-why-starting-feels-impossible/) [OT4ADHD](https://ot4adhd.com/2022/10/18/task-initiation-and-adhd-strategies-and-support-for-getting-started/) Steps must feel doable *for the in-the-moment brain*, not the idealized well-rested one (ADHD coaching consensus).
- **Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer, peer-reviewed):** "If situation X, then I will do Y" plans automate initiation. [National Cancer Institute](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/research/constructs/implementation-intentions) Studied in children with ADHD (Gawrilow & Gollwitzer, *Cognitive Therapy and Research*) with positive effects; incorporated into Ramsay & Rostain's adult CBT as "If X, Then Y." A meta-analysis (Toli et al., 2016, *British Journal of Clinical Psychology*) supports if-then planning in clinical samples.
- **Backward planning / SMART adaptations:** SMART goals adapted for ADHD emphasize *achievable* (start smaller than feels necessary, to counter all-or-nothing thinking) and *time-bound* (distant deadlines barely register), paired with external tracking. Goal Attainment Scaling is used as an outcome in ADHD trials.
- **Readiness/confidence ratings:** Scaling confidence (0–10) that a task will get started/done is drawn from motivational interviewing (used in Safren's protocol) and identifies tasks needing further breakdown (low confidence → chunk more).
- **Time-blindness interventions (Barkley; Tuckman):** Externalize time (analog clocks, visual/countdown timers at the point of performance); shorten time horizons; reframe "now vs. not now"; apply buffer multipliers (~1.5×; percentage rather than fixed) for the planning fallacy; and measure recurring tasks a few times to calibrate. [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/time-estimation-planning-fallacy-adhd/) Ari Tuckman (concepts of *time horizon* and *temporal discounting*) recommends learning to "see time by externalizing it, and feel time by building motivation."

### 4. Operationalization in Obsidian + TaskNotes

TaskNotes stores each task as a Markdown note with YAML frontmatter. It supports native Obsidian `#tags`, `@contexts`, `+projects`, priority, status, due vs. scheduled dates, [Tasknotes](https://tasknotes.dev/settings/task-properties/) a `timeEstimate` field (minutes; NLP parses "30min", "2h", "1h30m"), time-tracking (start/stop, Pomodoro, [Obsidian Stats](https://www.obsidianstats.com/plugins/tasknotes) and estimated-vs-actual statistics), recurrence, dependencies, and **user-defined frontmatter fields** (e.g., `effort`, `energy`, `client`) that become filterable/groupable in Bases. Natural-language capture extracts tags/contexts/dates/estimates inline (e.g., `Buy groceries tomorrow #errands`). Custom fields can be triggered during capture (e.g., `effort: high`). This maps cleanly onto the taxonomy below, and priority values should be named to sort correctly in Bases (e.g., `1-urgent`, `2-high`).

## Recommendations

### A synthesized ADHD task taxonomy (adapt freely)

Use a small number of orthogonal dimensions. Each maps to a documented deficit and drives a specific initiation strategy. Keep the controlled vocabulary short — decision fatigue is itself an ADHD tax.

**Dimension 1 — Activation difficulty (initiation energy).** *Grounds: Brown's Activation cluster; "activation energy"/Wall of Awful.* Tags: `#act/low` `#act/med` `#act/high`. Rule: any `#act/high` task must have a `nextstep::` field with a ≤2-minute physical first action and an implementation intention.

**Dimension 2 — Executive-function demand.** *Grounds: Brown/Barkley/Solanto.* Tags: `#ef/planning` `#ef/organizing` `#ef/focus` `#ef/memory` `#ef/emotion`. Signals which support to pre-stage (checklist, template, body double).

**Dimension 3 — Structure.** *Grounds: OT task-demand analysis.* `#struct/defined` (clear steps) vs. `#struct/open` (ambiguous). Open-ended tasks get a mandatory "define the deliverable" pre-step.

**Dimension 4 — Time estimate + buffer.** *Grounds: timing-deficit meta-analyses (Marx 2022; Zheng 2022); planning fallacy.* Use TaskNotes `timeEstimate` (raw gut estimate) plus a `timeBudget::` = estimate × 1.5 (round up). Duration bucket tags for triage: `#dur/quick` (≤15m) `#dur/short` (15–45m) `#dur/long` (45m–2h) `#dur/project` (>2h → must be broken down; a `#dur/project` is never actionable as-is).

**Dimension 5 — Energy / cognitive load.** *Grounds: OT energy-pattern matching.* `#load/light` `#load/heavy`. Schedule `#load/heavy` in your medication/peak window; batch `#load/light` for low-energy times.

**Dimension 6 — Interest/urgency (motivational levers).** *Grounds: Dodson ICNU (heuristic).* `#mot/interest` `#mot/novel` `#mot/urgent` `#mot/dull`. A `#mot/dull` + `#act/high` combination is the classic paralysis zone → pair with body doubling, a timer, or a reward.

**Dimension 7 — Life domain / context.** Use TaskNotes `@contexts`: `@work` `@home` `@errands` `@calls` `@social` `@admin`. Contexts enable "do all `@errands` in one trip" batching.

### Achievability rating scheme (capture-time, ~30 seconds)
Rate two numbers, stored as frontmatter:
- `clarity::` 1–5 — do I know exactly what "done" looks like and what the first action is? (<3 → break down before scheduling.)
- `confidence::` 0–10 — how confident am I that I'll actually start this when scheduled? (<7 → chunk further, add an if-then plan, or attach an external cue/body double.)

Combine into a simple gate: **Ready = clarity ≥3 AND confidence ≥7 AND has a `nextstep::`**. Only "Ready" tasks go on the day's scheduled list; everything else stays in a triage bin.

### Time estimation scheme
1. Record gut `timeEstimate`. 2. Auto/manually set `timeBudget = estimate × 1.5`. 3. Use TaskNotes time-tracking to log actuals. 4. Weekly, review estimated-vs-actual in a Base and recalibrate your personal multiplier per task type. 5. For recurring tasks, replace the multiplier with your measured median.

### Capture → Triage → Initiate workflow
1. **Capture (frictionless):** inline `- [ ]` or NLP capture: `Draft budget memo tomorrow #work #ef/planning #act/high 90min`. Don't rate yet.
2. **Triage (daily/weekly, batched):** for each new task set `clarity`, `confidence`, `timeEstimate`/`timeBudget`, energy, and a `nextstep::`. Break any `#dur/project` or clarity<3 into child tasks. This is the executive-function-heavy step — do it in your peak window, externalizing everything onto the page.
3. **Initiate (point of performance):** pull only "Ready" tasks matching current `@context` and energy; start a visible timer/Pomodoro; execute the `nextstep::` if-then plan.

### Staged rollout (don't build it all at once)
- **Week 1–2:** Only three fields — `timeEstimate`, a 1.5× `timeBudget`, and a `nextstep::`. Prove the estimate/actual loop works.
- **Week 3–4:** Add `#act/*` and `#dur/*` tags and the clarity/confidence ratings; start a "Ready" filtered Base view.
- **Month 2:** Add energy/EF/interest dimensions and weekly recalibration.
- **Thresholds to change course:** if you stop tagging within two weeks, the vocabulary is too big — cut back to activation + `timeEstimate` + `nextstep`. If estimates converge on actuals, lower your multiplier. If the "Ready" list is chronically empty, your chunking step is failing — make next-steps smaller.

## Caveats
- **Evidence tiers matter.** Barkley's model, the timing-deficit meta-analyses (Marx 2022; Zheng 2022), the CBT RCTs (Safren 2010, *JAMA*; Solanto 2010, *Am J Psychiatry*), and implementation-intentions research are peer-reviewed. Dodson's INCUP/ICNU, the "Wall of Awful," dopamine menus, and the specific 1.5× buffer multiplier are clinical heuristics or practitioner rules of thumb — useful, but not validated constructs. Treat them as scaffolding, not established fact.
- **Population caveat:** several implementation-intention and some timing studies are in children; adult-specific replication is thinner, though Ramsay & Rostain incorporate if-then planning clinically with adults, and the timing meta-analyses include adult samples.
- **A system is not a treatment.** Tools externalize executive function but don't replace medication, therapy, or coaching, which carry the strongest evidence. The tagging system itself imposes EF load; if maintaining it becomes another avoided task, radically simplify.
- **Individual variability is high.** OT literature stresses that a system that works brilliantly for one ADHD person may be unusable for another; calibrate to your own energy patterns and to where *your* breakdown actually occurs.
- **Beware over-engineering.** The single most common failure mode for PKM-enthusiast ADHDers is building an elaborate system as a form of productive procrastination. The minimum viable version — estimate + buffer + next step + visible timer — delivers most of the benefit.
